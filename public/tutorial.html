WordBreak

Boggle, except finding a word doesn’t end it — it starts a fight over it.

The loop

Trace a word through adjacent letters. The moment your trail spells a word you claim it, and those letters are locked to you. Survive the hold time and the claim banks: one point per letter, the tiles vanish, and fresh letters reseed in their place.

But a claim is only as strong as it is long. Anyone — including you — can break it with a longer word through the same letters.

Claiming

Click letters in sequence, or press and drag across them. Any two tiles touching orthogonally or diagonally are adjacent, and no tile can be used twice in one word. There is no three-letter minimum: DO is a perfectly good claim.

Your trail stays live after it claims, so you can keep reaching. Trace D → O and you hold DO; add G and you hold DOG; add S and you hold DOGS. Each step replaces the last — and restarts the hold clock. A DOG that is one second from banking is often worth more than a fresh DOGS.

Breaking

To use a letter inside someone else’s claim, the word you end up with must be strictly longer than that claim. Four letters beats three. Four does not beat four.

Breaking is all-or-nothing: touch one tile of a claim and the whole claim dies, because a claim is a word, not a pile of letters. The player you broke gets nothing — their hold time is simply lost.

Banking

Hold a claim for the full hold time and it converts. You score one point per letter, the tiles fly to your name, and new letters flip in.

What this means in practice

  • Long words are safe but slow to pay. A seven-letter claim is nearly unbreakable — an opponent needs eight through the same letters.
  • Short claims are almost free to take. Two-letter words lock very little, so use them to hold ground, not to score.
  • Contested claims never bank. Points come from words nobody bothers to beat, so the quiet corner of the board is often worth more than the crowded one.
  • Extending is a real decision. More letters, but a fresh clock and a louder signal to everyone watching that you’re building there.

House rules of this prototype

  • One round, no end condition — play until you’ve had enough.
  • Words come from ENABLE, minus anything containing q (there is no Q tile).
  • Letters are drawn on Boggle’s own frequencies, with a floor on vowels so the board can’t dry out.
  • Everything lives in memory on one server, so a deploy or restart ends the game.
